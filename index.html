01 · The brief

Four audiences,
one question each.

SDG 7 commits every country to universal access to affordable, reliable and modern energy by 2030, and to a substantially larger share of renewables. The dashboard turns two decades of country data into answers to the questions the people funding that goal actually ask.

Who asks what

Governments & NGOsWhere are the deepest gaps in electricity and clean cooking, and are they closing?
Development banksWhere does each new connection reach the most people, and will the power be clean?
Energy investorsWhere is demand growing faster than supply and investment?
Development economistsHow do income, urbanisation and political stability move with access?

Three design choices

  • A story, not a data dumpThree pages in order: the Overview sets out both targets, The Capture covers access (Target 7.1) and The Outliers covers renewables (Target 7.2).
  • Filters that matterA 2000–2022 year range with a play axis, a region to sub-region hierarchy, income group and political stability on every page.
  • Detail on demandFive tooltip pages show each country's income class, access rate and electricity sources on hover, so the maps stay clean.

02 · How I built it

Seven public datasets,
one story in three pages.

Excel Power Query shapes the raw downloads, a star-style model in Power BI joins them by country and year, and DAX turns them into leaderboards, people counts and map bins.

03 · What the data says

Six findings for
the people funding access.

Data

Electricity access from the World Bank; electricity by source and population from Our World in Data. 2022 unless noted.

Definitions

Gains are in percentage points. "Renewable-led" means at least half of a country's electricity comes from renewables.

Read with care

A share of electricity describes the grid, not the people off it, and a small grid can swing on a single power plant.
01

Twenty-two years, two speeds.

The fastest electrifiers went from a fifth or less of people to near-universal access: East Timor 17.8% → 99.7%, Cambodia 16.6% → 92.3% and Afghanistan 4.4% → 85.3%. The five lowest-access countries gained only 8 to 10 points over the same years and hold 60 million people without power between them.

Gains can also reverse. Afghanistan reached 97.7% in 2021, then lost 12.4 points in 2022.

So whatUniversal access within a generation is possible. Where it stalls, the constraint is delivery and stability, so plans need protection against shocks as well as funding.
02

The gap has an address.

In 2022, 22 countries had electricity for fewer than half their people. Twenty are in Sub-Saharan Africa; the others are Haiti and Papua New Guinea. Together they hold 353 million people without power, about half of the world's 685 million.

Rates and people point to different places. DR Congo's 21.5% is not among the five lowest rates, yet it leaves 80 million people without electricity, more than the five lowest-access countries combined. By 2024, 563 million of the 655 million people without power lived in Sub-Saharan Africa.

So whatMission 300, the World Bank and African Development Bank drive to connect 300 million people in Sub-Saharan Africa by 2030, targets the right region. The test is whether it reaches both the lowest rates and the largest numbers.
03

Clean cooking is the bigger gap.

In 2022, 2.1 billion people still cooked with polluting fuels, three times the 685 million without electricity. Access to clean cooking stood at 74%, against 91% for electricity. Under current policies, about 1.8 billion will still lack clean cooking in 2030.

The cost is counted in lives: household air pollution from polluting fuels causes 3.2 million premature deaths a year.

So whatElectricity programmes alone won't meet Target 7.1. Every connection plan needs a clean-cooking plan beside it: LPG supply, electric cooking on new connections and better stoves.
04

Ten of the 22 already run on renewables.

This is the dashboard's "outliers" story in numbers. Of the 22 countries below 50% access, 10 generate at least half their electricity from renewables: the Central African Republic and DR Congo 100%, Uganda 97%, Malawi 96% and Sierra Leone 95%. In all ten, hydropower supplies two-thirds or more.

The other 12 run mostly on fossil fuels, led by Guinea-Bissau (100%), South Sudan (98%), Niger (97.5%) and Chad (95%).

So whatIn the renewable-led ten, every new connection can be clean if supply keeps up. In the fossil-led twelve, solar mini-grids and home systems are the way to leapfrog diesel.
05

The renewable lead is slipping.

Of the 21 low-access countries with data for 2000, 9 now get a smaller share of their electricity from renewables, as demand outgrew hydro and the gap was filled with fossil fuels. Tanzania fell from 86.4% to 30.4%, Madagascar from 64.0% to 35.2% and Burundi from 100% to 69.2%.

Ten moved the other way, led by Sierra Leone (11.1% → 95.0%), Liberia (0% → 33.3%) and Guinea (50.6% → 74.5%).

So whatLeapfrogging is not automatic. Tie new connections to new renewable supply, or rising access will lock in diesel and gas.
06

Read every share with the size of the grid.

The dashboard's three top renewable improvers mean three different things. Sierra Leone went from 11.1% to 95.0% on a grid that generates about 0.2 TWh a year. Lithuania rose from 3.1% to 74.8% mainly because its nuclear plant closed: nuclear supplied 75.7% in 2000, generation fell from 11.1 to 4.0 TWh, and the fossil share rose. Denmark went from 15.5% to 81.5% on a steady 35 TWh, a genuine switch away from fossil fuels.

Worldwide, renewables' share of electricity rose from 18.7% to 29.5% over the same period.

So whatA percentage alone can't rank countries. Show generation and what was displaced next to every share before calling a country a leader.

04 · The next 12 months

Connect the most people,
keep new power clean.

Each action has an owner, a reason from the data and one measure to track.

The remaining gap is rural, it includes cooking, and new supply decides whether access stays clean. Three moves for energy ministries and regulators.

01

Publish a least-cost electrification plan

Do

Assign every unconnected area to grid extension, a mini-grid or solar home systems, with a date and a budget.

Why

In Sub-Saharan Africa, over 80% of urban residents had electricity in 2022, against 31% of rural residents. The remaining gap is mostly rural, where off-grid is often the cheaper route.

TrackNew connections per quarter, by type
02

Set a clean-cooking target beside every electricity target

Do

Fund LPG distribution, electric cooking on new connections and better stoves, each with its own target.

Why

2.1 billion people lacked clean cooking in 2022, three times the electricity gap, and household air pollution causes 3.2 million premature deaths a year.

TrackClean-cooking access rate
03

Make new supply renewable by default

Do

Run solar, hydro and storage tenders before approving new diesel or heavy-fuel-oil plants.

Why

9 of 21 low-access countries lost renewable share since 2000; Tanzania fell from 86.4% to 30.4%.

TrackRenewable share of new capacity

Mission 300 has the money and the mandate. Point it at the deepest gaps, keep it clean and protect what it builds.

04

Weight Mission 300 toward the lowest five

Do

Reserve connections for South Sudan, Burundi, Chad, Malawi and the Central African Republic.

Why

They hold 60 million people without power and gained only 8–10 points in 22 years.

TrackConnections in countries below 20% access
05

Go off-grid where the grid runs on fossil fuels

Do

Prioritise solar mini-grids and home systems in Chad, Niger, South Sudan and Guinea-Bissau.

Why

Their grids run on 95–100% fossil fuels, so extending them adds diesel demand.

TrackShare of new connections that are renewable
06

Protect gains in fragile states

Do

Ring-fence maintenance funds and off-grid support where conflict or political change threatens service.

Why

Afghanistan's access fell from 97.7% in 2021 to 85.3% in 2022: 12.4 points lost in one year.

TrackYear-on-year change in access, fragile states

The demand is large and the public money is moving. Size markets by people, diversify hydro and invest alongside the development banks.

07

Size markets by people, not rates

Do

Rank markets by people without power and urban growth before looking at the access rate.

Why

DR Congo has 80 million people without electricity at 21.5% access; Tanzania has 35 million and Uganda 25 million.

TrackAddressable households per market
08

Add solar and storage to hydro-heavy grids

Do

Back solar-plus-storage projects in the ten renewable-led, low-access markets.

Why

Hydropower supplies two-thirds or more of electricity in all ten, so a dry year hits supply.

TrackNon-hydro renewable capacity added
09

Co-invest with Mission 300 partners

Do

Use World Bank and African Development Bank guarantees and blended finance to enter the lowest-access markets.

Why

By mid-2026 the two banks had committed nearly $15 billion and Mission 300 had connected over 50 million people in 40 countries.

TrackProjects co-financed with Mission 300 partners

A monitoring dashboard is only as useful as its latest year. Keep it current, add the people and measure what users get.

10

Refresh with each Tracking SDG 7 release

Do

Extend the data to 2024 from the 2026 release and re-run every leaderboard.

Why

By 2024, 655 million people still lacked electricity, 563 million of them in Sub-Saharan Africa.

TrackData year shown on every page
11

Show people next to every percentage

Do

Add people without access, and generation in TWh, beside every rate and share.

Why

DR Congo's 21.5% access leaves 80 million without power; Sierra Leone's 95% renewable share runs on a 0.2 TWh grid.

TrackVisuals that show an absolute number
12

Track reliability, not just connections

Do

Add hours of supply and outage data where they exist, and tariffs to show affordability.

Why

SDG 7 asks for affordable, reliable and modern energy; a connection that fails every evening doesn't meet it.

TrackCountries with reliability data

05 · Long-term strategy, to 2030 and beyond

Universal, clean,
and built to last.

The 12-month actions start the work, and three tracks carry it to 2030 and past it: close the access gap where it is deepest, make clean cooking a twin target and keep the renewable lead as demand grows.

Track
Now2026–2027
Next2028–2030
BeyondAfter 2030

Close the access gap

Governments, Mission 300 partners

Now · 2026–2027

Plans for the lowest five

Least-cost plans and compacts for the five countries below 16% access, home to 60 million people without power.

Next · 2028–2030

Off-grid by default

Mini-grids and solar home systems as the default for rural areas of the 22 countries below 50% access.

Beyond · after 2030

From connection to use

Power for clinics, schools and farms, with access measured by hours of reliable supply, not just a meter.

Clean cooking as a twin target

Energy and health ministries, NGOs

Now · 2026–2027

Count it with electricity

A clean-cooking target and budget in every national energy compact.

Next · 2028–2030

Fuel, stoves and finance

LPG supply chains, electric-cooking tariffs and stove finance to beat the 1.8 billion projected for 2030.

Beyond · after 2030

Cooking as a health measure

Report clean cooking alongside the 3.2 million deaths a year from household air pollution.

Keep new power renewable

Utilities, regulators, investors

Now · 2026–2027

Renewable by default

Tender rules that put solar, hydro and storage ahead of diesel in the 12 fossil-led, low-access countries.

Next · 2028–2030

Diversify hydro

Solar and storage in the ten hydro-led markets, so a dry year doesn't mean blackouts.

Beyond · after 2030

Regional power pools

Cross-border trade so hydro-rich and solar-rich grids can back each other up.

What I'd add to the dashboard next

How I build a policy dashboard

Lead with the decision

Each page answers one stakeholder question, and the cover tells readers where to go.

Show people next to percentages

DR Congo's 21.5% looks better than South Sudan's 8.4%, but it leaves 80 million people without power.

A share needs its denominator

Sierra Leone's 95% and Denmark's 81.5% rest on grids that differ in size more than a hundredfold.

Check the headline before it ships

Every cover number is re-run against the source before the dashboard goes out.
